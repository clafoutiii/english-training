3. How the Internet of Things works

The IoT is not one object. It is a network of connected devices that collect information, communicate online and trigger an action.

sensor
collects data
internet connection
shares data
smart device
decides or receives a command
action
switches on, reminds, orders

Useful patterns

allow + object + to + verb
The system allows devices to communicate.

tell + object + to + verb
The sensor tells the device to switch on.

Useful everyday vocabulary

= start making a sound

= have none left

Read and complete the connected-home notes

At 6:30, Noor’s alarm goes off. Her home network receives the signal and opens the blinds. It also tells the coffee maker to start. Small sensors in the refrigerator check what is inside. If the family is running out of milk, the fridge sends a shopping reminder to a phone. In a factory, similar devices can communicate when a machine needs attention. Doctors can also receive information from wearables, such as smart watches, to monitor a patient.

6. H/W — semi-formal email of congratulations

Write your own email, modelled on the organisation of the email from Unit 6c, Exercise 7. This is the only extended writing task in the lesson and will be included in your final report for feedback.

Situation: A former colleague, Alex, has started a new job at a company that develops smart devices for homes. Write to congratulate Alex. Use a friendly, semi-formal tone.
  1. Opening: greet Alex, say it has been a while, and congratulate them on the new job.
  2. Strengths: mention two work values or skills that make Alex suitable for the role.
  3. Challenge: say what may be demanding about the job and why Alex will manage well. Use one modal of possibility or advice.
  4. Closing: give a wish for the future and sign off naturally.

Target: 120–160 words. Try to use at least two technology terms from this lesson and two work-value words.
