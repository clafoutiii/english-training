1. Meaning first: how sure are you?

A deduction is a conclusion based on evidence. You do not know the fact; you make a logical guess.

almost impossible
almost certain
can’t / couldn’tmay / might / couldmust

Strong positive deduction

must = “I am almost sure this is true.”

Strong negative deduction

can’t / couldn’t = “I am almost sure this is not true.”

Possible deduction

may / might / could = “This is possible, but I am not sure.”

Do not confuse the meanings

must can mean an obligation: I must finish this report. Here it means a deduction: She must be busy. The evidence makes the difference.

2. Form: present or past?

First decide when the situation happened. Then choose the pattern.

Now / general situation

modal + base verb

He must work long hours.

modal + be + -ing

He might be working now.

→

Finished past situation

modal + have + past participle

He must have worked late yesterday.

modal + have been + -ing

He might have been working when you called.

Common B1 mistakes
  • After a modal, use the base verb: might work, not might works or might to work.
  • For a past deduction, add have + V3: can’t have left, not can’t left.
  • Use can’t, not mustn’t, for a negative deduction. Mustn’t means “it is forbidden”.

Why? A modal is followed by a base verb; past deductions need have + V3; use can’t for a logical negative conclusion.

3. Job language: useful chunks

Learn complete phrases, not isolated words. Click the dotted items while you read.

“The work is worthwhile, even when it is difficult.”

“Nursing can be demanding, but it is rewarding.”

Use it before a noun or an -ing form.

Useful for people, problems and situations.

A formal alternative to “be able to”.

A work arrangement, not a personal quality.

Extra hours beyond normal working hours.

A career step to a higher position.

Strongly negative; use with care.

Optional model answer — open after writing

A job I would consider is event coordination. It might be demanding before a large event because there are many details to check. An event coordinator is responsible for dealing with clients and suppliers, so they must be organised and reliable. They may need to work overtime sometimes, but the job can be worthwhile when an event goes well. A person in this role must possess an ability to stay calm under pressure. It cannot be a monotonous job because every event is different. I would also value training and a clear chance to get a promotion.
